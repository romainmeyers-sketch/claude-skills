# SteerCo Deck Playbook

A reference guide for preparing executive Steering Committee (SteerCo)
materials. The goal is to consistently build decks that facilitate leadership
decisions, highlight tradeoffs, and focus discussion on executive actions
rather than workstream updates.

The seven rules below are the source of truth. Each is followed by how it shows
up in a well-built SteerCo and the failure mode it prevents.

---

## 1. Start with the leadership conversation

- Frame the deck around the decisions leaders need to make, not around work
  completed.
- Ensure each section naturally leads to a discussion or executive input.
- Move supporting analysis and detailed work to the appendix.

**In practice:** The main deck is a short arc — where we are, what puts the plan
at risk, and the specific decisions we need. Work completed is evidence for the
ask, not the point of the slide.

**Failure mode:** A status report. If leaders can nod along and leave without
deciding anything, the deck failed.

---

## 2. Build for executive readability

- Every slide answers the question: "So what?"
- Lead with the implication, risk, dependency, or decision — not the process.
- Keep messages concise and focused on business impact.

**In practice:** The first line of every slide is the conclusion. Process and
method sit beneath it or in the appendix.

---

## 3. Maintain a consistent story

- Use the same organizational levels and terminology throughout the deck.
- Apply a consistent slide structure across sections to reduce cognitive load.
- Ensure visuals and labels reinforce the same narrative.

**In practice:** If you call them "value streams" on slide 2, they are "value
streams" on slide 20. Tables that recur (risk, decision) keep the same columns.

---

## 4. Surface risks and tradeoffs

- Explicitly identify the risks that require leadership support.
- Clearly communicate tradeoffs, dependencies, and constraints.
- Highlight what happens if key decisions are delayed.

**In practice:** Every risk carries an **implication** ("if this slips, the
first PI becomes a refinement exercise, not planning") and resolves to a
**named ask** on the Leadership Decisions slide. Protecting one thing usually
costs another — say what.

---

## 5. Keep the main deck focused

- Include only information required for executive discussion.
- Relocate implementation details, workplans, and supporting artifacts to the
  appendix.
- Use summaries, heatmaps, and concise visuals instead of detailed activity
  lists where appropriate.

**In practice:** Main deck = summary + risks + decisions (+ one tradeoffs slide
if needed). Pod workplans, org charts, operating-model diagrams, SAFe
references, and per-pilot deep dives all live behind the Appendix divider.

---

## 6. Write executive titles

- Titles communicate the key message or implication rather than describe the
  content.
- Use outcome-oriented language that immediately explains why the slide matters.
- Summarize the conclusion, not the topic. (The "so what.")

**Topic title -> Executive title:**

| Topic (weak) | Executive (strong) |
|---|---|
| Team Structure | Protecting Pilot Capacity Creates Real Tradeoffs |
| Dependencies | The Pilot Can Launch Only If These Dependencies Land in Sequence |
| Risks | Leadership Decisions Needed for Pilot Effectiveness |
| Status Update | The Pilot Is On Track to Launch, With Three Decisions Needed From You |

**Test:** Read only the slide titles top to bottom. If they tell the whole
story and imply the asks, the titles are doing their job.

---

## 7. Final quality check

Before sending, confirm:

- [ ] Is the requested leadership action clear on every slide that has one?
- [ ] Does every slide support the overall narrative?
- [ ] Could someone understand the key message by reading only the slide titles?
- [ ] Have unnecessary operational details been moved out of the main story?
- [ ] Is the deck easy to follow from beginning to end?
- [ ] Does the anchor slide (Achieved / Working On / What's Next) stand on its
      own as a one-slide summary?
- [ ] Does each risk resolve to a specific, owned decision?
- [ ] No em dashes; Metis brand colors and Calibri throughout; no shadows.
