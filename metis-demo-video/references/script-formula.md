# Script formula

Distilled from ten strong launch and product videos (YC launches, Loom, Linear, Mocha, Lovable,
Lovio/Zelios-style SaaS promos). They differ in look but share one shape:

| # | Beat | Length | Job | Scene type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | 4 to 6 s | A pain question or a surprising fact the viewer recognises | `hook` |
| 2 | **Old way** | 6 to 12 s | Show the current tool and what it costs (time, errors, invisibility) | `pages`, `quote` |
| 3 | **Meet X** | 4 to 5 s | Name + one-line promise | `reveal` |
| 4 | **Proof** | 35 to 55 s | 3 to 5 features, each one shown working, each with a caption | `app` |
| 5 | **How / why** | 6 to 10 s | What makes it possible or why it matters (cost, ownership, speed) | `cards` |
| 6 | **Close** | 4 to 6 s | One memorable line, or a call to action | `statement` |

Rules that make the difference:

- **Start with the viewer's problem, not the product.** "Still tracking your rollout in a Word
  document?" beats "Introducing the Team Guide".
- **Every feature = claim + proof.** The caption makes the claim; the camera shows it happening
  (click, result appears, zoom on the result).
- **One idea per caption, one caption per camera move.** If you need two sentences, it is two steps.
- **Real moments over feature lists.** "A teammate checks something and everyone sees it" with a
  live notification is stronger than a list of five features.
- **Name the people** when it is a client story (with permission): a real quote from the person
  who asked for it makes the problem credible.
- **End on the bigger idea**, not on credits. "A consulting deliverable, turned into a tool teams
  actually use."
- Write for an audience: for Metis colleagues, stress reuse and speed ("same pattern for any
  checklist"); for a client, stress their outcome and ownership.
- No em dashes, no "revolutionary", "seamless" or "unlock". Plain words.

Before building, show the user a table: scene, on-screen text, what the camera shows, seconds.

Voiceover version of the same table: one spoken sentence per idea; the hook line and the "Meet X" line are spoken
too; the "old way" beat can carry three short sentences, one per pain point, each appearing as it is said; end on
the bigger idea, then a quiet credit line that is on screen only. See `voiceover.md` for how the picture follows the voice.
